# J40 Cooling-Pack Restoration and Chassis-Mounting Plan

> **SUPERSEDED FOR THE NATURALLY ASPIRATED BUILD.** This document preserves the former Rev L turbo/K0/twin-front-fan plan for traceability. Do not use it for fabrication or purchasing. Use [J40 Naturally Aspirated Cooling + A/C Restoration Guide — Rev M](J40-naturally-aspirated-cooling-pack-restoration-guide-rev-m-20260814.md), which removes K0 and the second front fan while retaining the large rear radiator puller.

**Issue:** 14 August 2026
**Status:** **SUPERSEDED HISTORICAL PLAN — NOT FOR FABRICATION OR PURCHASING**
**Applies to:** R0 engine radiator, C0 A/C condenser, G0 stone guard, FL large rear electric fan/shroud, FS small front-fan candidate, the existing chassis support arms, and all fittings needed to mount them correctly
**Controlled basis:** [Rev L cooling-pack specification](J40-photo-measured-retained-cooling-pack-specification-rev-l.md) and [Rev L complete parts list](j40-rev-l-complete-oem-donor-parts-list-20260803.md)

Rev L supersedes the earlier Rev K and older radiator-workstream layouts. In particular, FL is an **electric rear puller**, not a mechanical fan, and all photo-derived dimensions remain mock-up values until checked on the real vehicle.

## 1. Intended result

Restore the retained parts to a clean, straight and coordinated OEM-style finish without sacrificing heat transfer, pressure integrity, isolation or service access:

- straight, clean and uniformly presented heat-exchanger faces;
- deep satin or restrained semi-gloss black on visible brackets, shrouds and guard;
- matching bright-zinc automotive fasteners, aligned washers and neat witness marks;
- fresh black EPDM rubbers, hoses, seals, edge trim and anti-chafe material;
- tidy sealed fan wiring in black loom with properly spaced rubber-lined P-clips;
- no overspray, loose mesh, exposed rust, mismatched hardware, unsupported pipes or visible improvised mounting legs.

“Gleaming” means meticulous and corrosion-free, not heavy gloss paint on cores. Thick paint, powder coat or cosmetic filler must never be applied to radiator or condenser fins, soldered seams, plastic fan blades, motor vents, electrical labels, hose lands, sealing faces or rubber isolators.

The controlled finished air path remains:

**grille → G0 stone guard → two equal front pushers → reserved low-centred K0 intercooler position → C0 condenser → R0 radiator → sealed FL rear puller/shroud → engine**

K0 is a new component rather than a restoration item, but its full dummy, clearances and independent mounts must be represented during every pack dry fit.

## 2. Retain, restore or replace decision

| Item | Starting decision | Release condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R0 copper/brass radiator** | Restore original tanks, filler, necks, rails, ears and lower locators where sound; the budget and programme should assume a recore because the photographed core is tired and damaged. | Retain or copy each original piece only after inspection; completed unit must pass pressure, flow and dimensional checks. |
| **C0 aluminium A/C condenser** | Restore the full-width condenser, pipes and brackets if serviceable. The old receiver/drier remains a geometry sample only. | Pass external-fin, flush/contamination and specialist pressure/leak checks. Fit a new compatible drier and new disturbed-joint seals. |
| **G0 stone guard** | Restore the frame; straighten or renew the expanded-mesh insert to the original open-area pattern. | Frame is square, mesh has no loose/broken strands, open area is preserved, and the complete guard is independently removable. |
| **FL large fan and full shroud** | Clean and restore only after its motor, blade, shroud and electrical performance pass. | Correct engineward pull, sound mounts/blade/shroud, acceptable start/run current, no bearing play or rubbing, and released installed airflow. |
| **FS small front fan** | Treat as the master sample and candidate, not an automatically accepted final part. | Use with one exact tested mate only if the complete pair fits and matches. Otherwise fit two identical replacement pushers and keep FS as a tested spare. |
| **Existing radiator arms/uprights** | Inspect and retain the July-welded structure if geometry and weld quality pass. | Weld/HAZ, height, plane, hole alignment, clearance, proof load and coating restoration all pass. |
| **Old added radiator leg** | Record its geometry, then remove it from the service configuration. | It is a historical workaround only. Do not reproduce it on R0 or use it to carry radiator weight. |

## 3. Stage 0 — evidence and measurements before stripping

Do this with R0, C0, G0, FL, FS and the vehicle physically together.

1. Attach durable tags `R0`, `C0`, `G0`, `FL` and `FS`; mark orientation, front/rear, left/right and vehicle centreline.
2. Photograph every face, label, plug, pipe, bracket, crack, solder joint and mounting point with a scale before dismantling.
3. Cap every open coolant and refrigerant connection immediately. Keep C0 dry and clean internally.
4. Record R0 true outside width/depth, cap height, active face, neck positions and diameters, drain, ear coordinates and lower-locator geometry.
5. Make one rigid **B0** template that captures both original upper chassis-hole diameters and XYZ centres.
6. Make one rigid **S0** template that captures both R0 lower locators, saddle centres, pin diameters and required installed rubber height.
7. Record the lower-crossmember clear span and end interfaces as **L0**, and weigh the filled and capped radiator as **MR**.
8. Record C0 complete width, height and depth including tabs, pipes, drier/manifold, ports, cable/pipe bends and A/C-tool sweep.
9. Record FL and FS complete envelopes, including every frame tab, guard, motor, plug and required cable bend. Blade/ring diameter alone is not a fit dimension.
10. Record the clear vehicle width at top, middle and bottom, both depth zones, bonnet/latch/grille/bumper envelopes, engine movement, hoses, belts, wiring and all service-tool sweeps.

No final ear, cradle, condenser bracket, fan carrier or guard fitting may be drilled, welded or coated until these measurements are on a dated drawing and the opaque 1:1 stack passes.

## 4. Component restoration work packages

### 4.1 R0 — original copper/brass engine radiator

**Specialist:** experienced copper/brass radiator shop.

1. Carry out baseline pressure and flow tests and provide written results before cosmetic work.
2. Inspect the top and bottom tanks, header joints, filler neck and cap seat, hose necks, overflow nipple, drain, side rails, ears and lower locators for cracking, dezincification, thinning, distortion and failed solder.
3. Use controlled radiator-shop chemical cleaning and gentle external cleaning. Do not abrasive-blast the assembled core or use aggressive pressure that folds fins.
4. Plan and quote a new core compatible with the Rev L 50 °C/150 bhp duty. The system release target is at least 115 kW continuous and 130 kW for 10 minutes; the actual core construction must be agreed from the real unit and test data, not a photograph.
5. Jig the retained tanks, rails, ears and locators before desoldering. Recore squarely without changing the neck map, cap height, drain access, FL sealing face or B0/S0 relationships.
6. Repair or reproduce only sound original ears and locators. After recording it, remove the added support leg/workaround without damaging the tank or rail; do not copy it.
7. Dress hose lands smooth and round, test the cap seat and drain, and fit a new approved-pressure cap only after the system pressure is released.
8. Repeat pressure and flow tests after all soldering and again after final external finishing if the coating process adds heat.
9. Mask the filler throat/cap seat, neck interiors and clamp lands, drain seat/threads, identification marks, rubber contacts and all fastener interfaces.
10. Apply only a thin radiator-shop heat-exchanger coating. Use an even satin/semi-gloss black on tanks and side rails, with the minimum compatible coating on the core. No powder coat, thick primer or body filler on the heat exchanger.

**R0 visual acceptance:** square tanks and rails; straight uniform core; clean soldering; no green residue or active corrosion; no runs or blocked fins; crisp masking; new cap, hoses and clamps; no improvised leg.

### 4.2 C0 — retained full-face A/C condenser

**Specialist:** automotive A/C shop familiar with aluminium condensers and the actual refrigerant/oil system.

1. Keep the ports capped. Identify refrigerant, oil, every fitting and seal type from the real system before opening or flushing.
2. Inspect manifolds, tubes, brackets, pipe unions, service access and every fin row. Reject the unit if corrosion, impact or contamination cannot be safely repaired.
3. Externally clean with condenser-safe neutral chemistry and low-pressure rinsing/air in the fin direction. Carefully comb folded fins without puncturing tubes.
4. Flush only by an approved A/C procedure. Never use oxygen, shop air or an unapproved solvent.
5. Pressure/leak-test with dry nitrogen or approved forming gas at the system/service-manual test pressure. Do not guess a pressure from the photographs.
6. Straighten or repair original tabs in a jig without loading the manifold or core. Any heat repair to aluminium stays with the specialist.
7. Use the photographed old drier/manifold to control packaging only. Fit a new compatible receiver/drier or service element, new HNBR O-rings/seals and new service caps at final assembly; keep the new drier sealed until installation.
8. Preserve the full face and all required pipe/tool sweeps. Do not crop C0, hang K0 from it or use its body as the front-fan carrier.
9. Leave sound natural aluminium clean or renew only the original-type ultra-thin heat-exchanger coating. Finish separate steel brackets in the black bracket system below. Do not powder-coat the core or bridge fins with paint.

**C0 visual acceptance:** clean and uniformly straight face; capped clean ports; crisp restored tabs; no bent pipe caused by mounting; new drier/seals bagged and identified; written leak-test result.

### 4.3 G0 — removable expanded-mesh stone guard

**Specialist:** fabricator/coater after mock-up release.

1. Photograph and jig the original frame, hole positions, offsets and curvature before straightening.
2. Degrease, remove the mesh if practical, strip loose coating and corrosion, and inspect the frame and welds for thinning or cracks.
3. Square the frame. Replace the ragged or broken mesh insert if straightening would leave loose strands; match the original mesh pitch, wire/strand size and open area.
4. Grind only sharp projections and untidy repair spatter. Do not thin the frame or close its drainage path.
5. Blast or mechanically prepare **the steel guard only**, not any heat exchanger. Apply epoxy primer followed by a durable 2K satin/semi-gloss black topcoat. Keep the mesh coating thin enough that apertures do not bridge.
6. Add neat black edge trim or anti-chafe material where the released assembly could touch an adjacent carrier. Use fresh rubber-faced stand-offs at the released mounting points.
7. Mount it with accessible captive or locking hardware on its independent carrier. It must come off without removing R0 or discharging the A/C.

**G0 visual acceptance:** square, taut and centred mesh; uniform open area; no sharp/broken strands; clean black finish on all visible edges; identical fasteners; no contact with fins or fan guards.

### 4.4 FL — large rear electric puller and full shroud

**Specialists:** auto electrician plus plastic/metal repairer as required.

1. Before dismantling, record manufacturer, part number, voltage, plug, polarity, rotation and airflow direction.
2. Check blade roots, blade runout, motor shaft/end play, bearing noise, guard clearance, shroud cracks and all mounting tabs. Reject a blade or shroud with unsafe cracking around the motor or support points.
3. Bench-record start current, stabilised current and voltage at the motor. Confirm that the installed orientation pulls air through R0 toward the engine in the blade/shroud’s designed direction.
4. Wash plastic with a plastic-safe detergent and soft brushes. Do not solvent-soak, blast or paint the blades, motor, vents, plug, label or rubber mounts.
5. Plastic-weld minor non-critical shroud cracks with compatible material and reinforcement on the hidden side. Replace the shroud if motor or load-bearing mounts cannot be restored reliably.
6. Clean and repaint separate steel brackets with epoxy primer and 2K black topcoat. Restore faded plastic only with a compatible thin plastic coating after adhesion testing; otherwise leave it clean and original.
7. Fit sound connector shells with new correctly crimped terminals and seals, new cable/earth sized from the measured current, black loom and proper strain relief.
8. Attach the full shroud through an independent removable support arrangement that does not make the radiator fins, tanks or soldered seams carry its weight. Seal the shroud perimeter to the released R0 face with closed-cell EPDM without covering fins, drainage or service points.

**FL visual acceptance:** clean matched-black appearance; no wobble, rub or vibration; protected label; sealed plug; tidy loom; removable shroud; documented current, direction and installed-airflow result.

### 4.5 FS — small front-fan candidate and its matching pair

1. Perform the same label, blade, bearing, current, polarity and airflow checks as FL before spending on cosmetics.
2. Take the complete FS module to the donor supplier. A retained FS plus one mate is permitted only when frame, tabs, guard, motor, blade, plug, label/part number, depth, current and direction truly match.
3. If an exact mate is unavailable or either fan fails, use two identical Toyota/Denso replacement modules and retain the tested FS as a labelled spare.
4. Restore the accepted pair together so both guards, shrouds, grommets, connectors and visible finish match. Do not cut either guard or mix different fan designs.
5. Fit both to one centred, removable carrier with donor-pattern rubber grommets/shoulder sleeves. The pair, carrier, plugs and cable bends must remain inside the released front-view envelope and must not hang from G0 or C0.
6. Give each front fan its own sealed relay, covered fuse and earth branch. Route equal-looking looms symmetrically and P-clip them clear of fins and service paths.

**Pair geometry acceptance:** preferred 10 mm centre gap; fan centres level and pair midpoint on vehicle centreline within 2 mm; equal positive side margins; both push grille-to-engine; no cropped guards or strained plugs.

## 5. Chassis-mount system and fittings

The R0 load path is mandatory:

**R0 lower locators → two new rubber saddles → full-width lower crossmember → gusseted/removable end connections → both structural side uprights**

The lower saddles carry all filled-radiator weight. The two upper fittings only locate R0 fore/aft and laterally.

| Ref | Qty | Fitting / fabricated item | Requirement |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| **X0** | 1 | Full-width lower crossmember | Start a fixture with 40 × 40 × 3 mm mild-steel SHS only if clear span is no more than 700 mm and MR is no more than 35 kg; otherwise recalculate. Provide drainage and removable/gusseted attachment to both uprights. |
| **X1** | 2 | Lower saddle seats | Level, reinforced seats fixed to X0 at the S0 centres. Their faces support the rubber saddles, not a tank or seam. |
| **R1** | 2 | New Toyota/J40-pattern lower rubber saddles | Sample-match locator diameter, cup depth, installed height, load area and seat. Both pins must sit fully without metal contact. |
| **T0** | 2 | Short removable upper radiator ears/stays | Drill from B0 only after R0 rests naturally on both R1 saddles. No slotting and no forced alignment. Finish as removable black brackets. |
| **R2** | 2 sets | Upper isolation hardware | New automotive EPDM bush, steel crush sleeve, large washers, zinc-plated class 8.8 bolt and locking nut at each original inward-facing chassis hole. Sleeve length controls compression; the bolt must not crush the rubber solid. |
| **A3** | To all released tabs | C0 isolator sets | New Toyota/Denso-pattern grommets or automotive EPDM, shoulder/crush sleeves, washers and removable locking fasteners. C0 has independent brackets and no metal-to-metal core/manifold load. |
| **F0** | 1 carrier set | Centred twin-front-fan carrier | Independent removable carrier with matched fan grommets/shoulder sleeves, symmetric holes and provisions for sealed plugs/loom. No through-core ties. |
| **FL0** | 1 support/seal set | Rear shroud attachment | Independent removable support plus closed-cell EPDM perimeter seal. No weight on fins, tanks, necks or solder seams. |
| **G1** | To released holes | Guard stand-offs and fasteners | Rubber-faced stand-offs/edge trim with captive or locking fasteners on the released independent carrier. No self-tappers into structural supports and no new chassis holes. |
| **M2** | 1 complete set | General hardware | New, matching zinc-plated class 8.8 bolts, large washers, locking nuts, correctly sized crush sleeves/spacers and any retained-component fasteners. Final diameters, pitches and lengths come from the released drawings. |
| **M3** | As measured | Sealing and protection | Closed-cell automotive EPDM foam, edge trim, anti-chafe strip, heat sleeve, grommets and rubber-lined metal P-clips. Keep all active fins, drainage and service points open. |

### Fastener workmanship standard

- Use the original two upper chassis holes and released existing pickup points. Do not slot, ream or drill new chassis holes and do not pull R0 into alignment with bolts.
- Do not use structural self-tappers, through-core plastic ties, ungraded bolts, stacked loose washers as spacers, or a fastener compressed through rubber without a crush/shoulder sleeve.
- Put bolt heads and washers in a consistent visible orientation; use one fastener finish and head style where practical.
- Set final torque from the released bolt size, grade, coating and joint design. Record it on the drawing, apply witness marks after inspection, and use the specified locking method rather than unrecorded thread compounds.
- Keep fasteners accessible with normal tools and arrange the pack so G0, fans, C0, R0 and FL can be removed in a logical sequence.

## 6. Existing chassis-arm and lower-cradle restoration

1. Clean the two welded radiator arms/uprights sufficiently to expose both weld toes, ends and heat-affected zones.
2. Inspect weld continuity, undercut, porosity, cracking and incomplete fusion; check both arms for matching height, plane, angle, hole alignment and clearance. Use non-destructive crack checking where visual findings are doubtful.
3. Do not grind structural welds flat for appearance. Repair only after geometry and load path are agreed by the competent fabricator.
4. Fit X0, X1 and temporary saddles; perform the complete bare-metal dry fit and proof load before any final coating.
5. Remove spatter and sharp edges, clear drains and protect all threads/mating faces.
6. Degrease and mechanically prepare sound steel; apply compatible epoxy primer. Match welded chassis pieces to the project chassis/Raptor top-protection system and finish removable visible carriers/brackets in durable 2K satin/semi-gloss black.
7. After cure, open every masked hole, confirm drains, apply cavity protection to eligible closed sections, then install fresh rubber and new hardware. Never coat rubber or trap it against wet paint.

## 7. Mandatory bare-metal dry fit before paint

Assemble an opaque full-size stack with the actual parts and a complete K0 dummy. The fit must represent the grille, bumper, bonnet/latch, pipes, hoses, wiring, fuse-box lid, tools and engine movement.

- R0 must sit naturally and level on both lower saddles with its top fittings loose; no tank, seam or fin may touch a support.
- Make and drill T0 only in that natural position. Tightening the top fittings must not lift R0 or move a lower locator in its saddle.
- Maintain the released separation between C0 and R0; Rev L uses 10–15 mm as the upper-zone fixture allowance.
- Prove cap removal, drain operation, upper/lower/overflow hose installation, A/C union and drier access, both fan plugs, fuse-lid sweep, belt/tool access and a credible core-removal path.
- Prove G0 removal without touching fins and prove each fan/condenser/shroud support is independent.
- Check all hoses and wires through the full engine-movement envelope. Nothing may rub a blade, belt, fin edge, bonnet/latch or sharp bracket.
- Verify that the two complete front fans, including plugs and cable bends, are centred and inside the released aperture.
- Seal bypass paths in the mock-up but do not block fins, drainage or the bonnet/latch mechanism.

Before paint, fill and cap R0, record MR, then proof-load X0/X1 at the two saddle centres to **twice MR for 10 minutes**. Pass requires no permanent set, cracked weld, loosened fastener, saddle movement or upright distortion.

## 8. Final assembly sequence

1. Install the cured lower crossmember, saddle seats and two new R1 saddles; verify level and S0 centres.
2. Lower the finished R0 into both saddles without scraping paint or loading a neck. Confirm full pin engagement.
3. Install both T0/R2 upper locating sets loose, settle the unit, then tighten to the released torque without compressing the isolation beyond the sleeves.
4. Install the independently supported C0 with new A3 isolators, leaving the new drier sealed until the A/C specialist completes the circuit.
5. Install the released K0 package when available, then the centred twin-fan carrier and G0. Maintain all dry-fit gaps.
6. Install FL and its perimeter seal at the rear. Hand-turn the blade and recheck all engine/hoses/tool clearances.
7. Fit new moulded upper, lower and overflow hoses, new constant-tension clamps, the approved cap, 2H thermostat/seal and compatible coolant service pack. Old hoses and the formed old pipe are patterns only where the replacement-pipe workstream applies.
8. Complete three independent fan branches with sealed relays, covered fuses, correct cable, grounds, terminals and strain relief. Keep every live stud covered and cable exits down/rear.
9. Complete the A/C with new drier/seals, evacuate, leak-test and recharge by the identified refrigerant mass and oil procedure.
10. Torque-check, witness-mark, clean fingerprints/residue, align clamps and P-clips, and photograph the finished assembly from grille and engine sides.

## 9. Release gates and acceptance record

### Gate A — specialist component release

- [ ] R0 pre-work and post-work pressure/flow results recorded.
- [ ] R0 retained-part/recore decision and final core construction recorded.
- [ ] C0 flush/contamination decision and nitrogen/forming-gas leak result recorded.
- [ ] New receiver/drier and all disturbed-joint seals identified.
- [ ] FL and both accepted front fans have label, polarity, direction, start current, stable current and voltage recorded.

### Gate B — structure and fit release

- [ ] B0, S0, L0, MR and full component/vehicle envelopes recorded on a dated drawing.
- [ ] Existing arm welds/HAZ and side-to-side geometry accepted.
- [ ] Opaque 1:1 stack passes bonnet, grille, bumper, latch, pipe, hose, wiring, service and engine-movement checks.
- [ ] Lower cradle passes the twice-MR/10-minute proof test before coating.
- [ ] Lower saddles carry the radiator; top fittings locate only and are neutral when loosened.
- [ ] Every heat exchanger, fan and guard has its own removable support; no through-core ties or improvised leg remain.

### Gate C — finish release

- [ ] No active rust, bare steel, coating run, overspray, bridged mesh aperture or blocked fin row.
- [ ] Heat-exchanger coating is thin and masking lines are clean.
- [ ] Rubber parts, labels, sealing faces, hose lands, connectors, motor vents and threads are paint-free.
- [ ] Visible fasteners match, wiring/hoses are aligned and supported, and all live terminals are covered.
- [ ] All drains and service/removal paths remain open after final assembly.

### Gate D — installed performance release

- [ ] Cooling and A/C circuits are leak-free through cold, hot-idle and heat-soak checks.
- [ ] FL pulls engineward; both front fans push engineward; no blade rub or abnormal vibration occurs.
- [ ] Complete installed stack delivers at least 3,000 m³/h at 13.5 V at fan terminals, with 3,300 m³/h preferred.
- [ ] Fan voltage drop, branch current, fuse/relay/cable temperature and charging performance pass at hot idle with normal loads.
- [ ] Instrumented Rev L 50 °C/150 bhp thermal test passes: R0 heat rejection, coolant stability, post-CAC IAT, charge pressure drop and A/C high-side stability meet the controlled criteria.
- [ ] Final inspection after the first full heat cycle finds no leaks, settling, witness-mark movement, hose strain, rubbing or loose fastener; repeat after the first loaded road test.

## 10. Shop handoff package

Give the shops the actual parts plus:

1. this restoration plan;
2. the controlled Rev L specification and parts list;
3. the B0/S0 rigid templates and dated measurement drawing;
4. pre-work photo sheets and the required acceptance checklist;
5. written instructions that all fabrication, drilling, proof testing and dry fit happen **before** final paint;
6. labelled bags for every original clip, bracket and fastener retained only as a pattern.

No part is accepted merely because it looks newly painted. A gleaming finish is the final layer over a pressure-tight, electrically proven, rubber-isolated and naturally aligned cooling pack.
