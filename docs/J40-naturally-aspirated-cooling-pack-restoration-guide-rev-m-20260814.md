# J40 Naturally Aspirated Cooling + A/C Restoration Guide — Rev M

**Issue:** 14 August 2026
**Status:** controlled shop guide; final measured drawings, fabrication release and installed performance release remain **HOLD**
**Applies to:** R0 copper/brass radiator, C0 full-face A/C condenser, G0 stone guard, one FS front A/C pusher, FL large rear radiator puller/shroud, chassis supports, hoses, fittings, wiring and finishes
**Decision:** naturally aspirated Toyota 2H; no turbocharger, no intercooler/K0, no charge-air pipework, and no second small front fan

This Rev M guide supersedes the turbo/K0/twin-front-fan arrangement in Rev L and the earlier restoration plan for this work scope. Rev L remains useful only as retained-part evidence and historical traceability. Photo-derived sizes are not fabrication dimensions.

## 1. Released architecture

The controlled air path is:

**grille → G0 removable stone guard → one centred FS A/C pusher → full-face C0 condenser → R0 radiator → sealed FL rear puller/shroud → engine**

- FS is the single front fan and serves condenser airflow during A/C operation.
- FL remains the primary radiator fan. Retain it only if its frame, motor, blade, current, direction and installed airflow pass.
- C0, FS, FL and G0 have independent removable supports. Nothing hangs from a heat-exchanger core.
- R0 alone is carried by its two lower locators in two new rubber saddles. The upper fittings locate and restrain only.
- A second front fan may be considered only under a future controlled revision if logged hot-idle A/C testing proves it necessary.

## 2. Non-negotiable hold points

Do not purchase final hoses, drill brackets, weld the final cradle or apply finish coating until all of the following are recorded from the actual vehicle and parts:

1. **B0:** rigid template of the two original upper chassis holes, including diameter and XYZ centres.
2. **S0:** rigid template of both R0 lower locators, saddle centres, pin diameters and required installed rubber height.
3. **L0/MR:** lower-crossmember clear span and filled/capped radiator mass.
4. **R0 map:** complete width, height, depth, active face, cap height, neck positions/diameters, drain, ears and locators.
5. **C0 map:** complete body plus tabs, pipes, receiver-drier/manifold, ports and tool sweep.
6. **Fan maps:** complete FS and FL frames, tabs, guards, motor depth, plugs and cable bends—not blade diameter alone.
7. **Vehicle envelope:** grille, bumper, bonnet/latch, support steel, hoses, A/C lines, engine movement, wiring and tool access.
8. An opaque 1:1 dry fit with the real parts passes before final cutting, drilling or coating.

## 3. Step-by-step shop sequence

### Step 1 — Identify, photograph and baseline-test

- Tag R0, C0, G0, FS and FL; mark front/rear, left/right and vehicle centreline.
- Photograph every face, label, port, plug, bracket, crack, solder joint and mounting point with a scale.
- Cap coolant and refrigerant ports immediately. Keep C0 dry internally.
- Record R0 baseline pressure and flow; C0 specialist leak/contamination condition; FS/FL start current, stabilised current, voltage, rotation and airflow direction.
- Reject unsafe fan blades, unrepairable shrouds, badly thinned tanks, cracked manifolds or structurally failed mounts before cosmetic work.

**Gate:** baseline records and retain/repair/replace decisions signed before stripping.

### Step 2 — Make templates and the measured release drawing

- Make B0 and S0 from rigid sheet or bar so they cannot rack.
- Record L0, MR and every envelope in Section 2 on one dated drawing with datum, tolerances and drawing revision.
- Use centreline and two fixed chassis datums; do not dimension a chain from bent sheet edges.
- Build an opaque full-size pack dummy and prove grille, bonnet/latch, cap, drain, hose, A/C union, drier, plug and tool access.

**Gate:** no photograph, render or nominal fan-ring size may substitute for the measured drawing.

### Step 3 — Restore R0 at a copper/brass radiator shop

1. Pressure- and flow-test before dismantling; inspect tanks, headers, necks, cap seat, overflow, drain, rails, ears and locators for thinning, cracks, dezincification and failed solder.
2. Jig the retained geometry. Budget for a new core because the photographed fins/core are tired; agree construction from the naturally aspirated duty, actual dimensions and test data.
3. Remove the historical added support leg after recording it. Do not reproduce it.
4. Clean with radiator-shop inhibited chemistry compatible with copper, brass and the actual solder. No abrasive blasting, strong DIY acid/caustic or uncontrolled pressure washing.
5. Recore/repair squarely while preserving the neck map, cap height, drain access, lower locators, B0/S0 relationship and FL sealing face.
6. Dress hose lands round and smooth. Repeat pressure and flow tests after all hot work.
7. Mask all openings, cap seat, threads, hose lands, labels and rubber contacts. Apply only a thin radiator-shop heat-exchanger coating; no powder coat, filler or thick primer on the core.

**Acceptance:** square, pressure-tight, free-flowing, clean solder, straight open fins, thin even finish and written test results.

### Step 4 — Restore C0 at an automotive A/C shop

1. Identify the installed refrigerant, oil and seal system before opening anything.
2. Keep ports capped; inspect tubes, manifolds, pipe unions, tabs and every fin row.
3. Use neutral aluminium/condenser-safe foaming cleaner, a low-pressure rinse from the clean side, and careful fin combing in the tube direction.
4. Flush only by an approved closed A/C procedure. Never use oxygen, wet shop air or an unidentified solvent.
5. Leak-test with dry nitrogen or approved forming gas at the applicable service pressure; do not invent pressure from photos.
6. Preserve the full face and all pipe/tool sweeps. Fit a new compatible receiver-drier, new HNBR seals/O-rings and service caps at final assembly; the old drier is a geometry sample only.
7. Leave a sound aluminium core clean unless the specialist approves an extremely thin compatible coating. Finish separate ferrous brackets under Step 8.

**Acceptance:** straight clean face, capped clean ports, no strained pipe, new sealed drier available and a written leak-test result.

### Step 5 — Restore G0 stone guard

- Jig the original frame, hole positions, offsets and curvature before repair.
- Remove loose coating and rust from the steel only; repair the frame without closing drainage.
- Straighten the mesh or replace it with matching pitch, strand size and open area if broken strands cannot be made safe.
- Apply 2K epoxy primer and a thin 2K polyurethane satin/semi-gloss black topcoat. Do not bridge mesh apertures.
- Fit black edge trim/EPDM anti-chafe where released, plus accessible locking fasteners on an independent carrier.

**Acceptance:** square frame, taut centred mesh, no sharp strands, clear apertures and removal without touching fins.

### Step 6 — Qualify and restore the two retained fan systems

For **FS**, retain exactly one complete small front module. Confirm it pushes air from grille toward engine in its designed orientation. Centre its complete frame—not merely its ring—on C0’s usable face, subject to ports, drier, brackets and tool clearance.

For **FL**, confirm the large complete rear module pulls air engineward in its designed orientation. Check blade roots/runout, shaft play, bearing noise, rub clearance, shroud cracks, tabs, label, plug and cable bend.

- Record start current and stabilised current at the motor terminals, supply voltage, polarity, rotation and direction.
- Wash with plastic-safe detergent and soft brushes. Do not blast, solvent-soak or paint blades, motor, vents, labels, plugs or rubbers.
- Plastic-weld only non-critical compatible shroud damage; replace any unsafe load-bearing or motor-support section.
- Renew sound connector housings with correctly crimped terminals and seals. Size cable, fuses and relays from measured run and inrush current.
- Give FS and FL separate protected branches, relays and earths.

**Acceptance:** no crack, wobble, rub, abnormal noise or unsafe current; documented direction/current and credible installed airflow.

### Step 7 — Fabricate the lower radiator cradle

Mandatory load path:

**R0 lower locators → two new Toyota/J40-pattern rubber saddles → formed saddle seats → full-width lower crossmember → both structural uprights**

- Treat **40 × 40 × 3 mm mild-steel SHS** only as a packaging/prototype starting section when **L0 ≤ 700 mm and MR ≤ 35 kg**. Those limits do not release it structurally. In every case, a competent automotive structural engineer or suitably qualified fabricator must document the design load cases and accept the crossmember, saddle seats, end connections, chassis interfaces, welds, fasteners and torque for vertical, fore/aft and lateral road loads, shock, vibration and fatigue. If either limit is exceeded, stop before using this provisional section even in the mock-up and calculate a new section.
- Form two level reinforced seats at the S0 centres. Each locator must fully seat in new rubber with no tank, seam or metal-to-metal contact.
- Connect the crossmember to both structural uprights through the released removable/gusseted details. Preserve drainage and service removal.
- Use only sound existing pickup structure. Inspect the July-welded arms, weld toes and heat-affected zones for cracking, porosity, undercut, fusion, height and plane before relying on them.

**Structural and proof gate before paint:** obtain the documented competent-person structural release above, then apply **2 × MR** at the two saddle centres for **10 minutes**. The proof supplements the calculation; it does not replace road-load or fatigue analysis. Pass means no permanent set, crack, loose fastener, saddle movement or upright distortion.

### Step 8 — Make the upper locators and independent carriers

- Let R0 settle naturally on both lower saddles. Make two short removable upper ears from the released structural drawing, then transfer/drill from B0.
- At each original upper hole use a new automotive EPDM bush, correctly fitted steel crush sleeve, broad washer, zinc-plated class 8.8 bolt and locking nut. Sleeve length—not bolt force—controls rubber compression.
- The upper ears must remain neutral: tightening must not lift R0, pull it sideways or unload either saddle.
- Make separate removable supports for C0, FS, FL/shroud and G0. Use EPDM isolators and shoulder/crush sleeves at released points.
- Seal FL’s perimeter to the R0 frame with closed-cell EPDM without obscuring fins, drainage, cap, drain or fasteners.

**Never:** slot/ream holes, drill new chassis holes, force parts into line, use through-core ties, structural self-tappers, loose washer stacks, unsleeved rubber joints or hang one heat exchanger from another.

### Step 9 — Bare-metal full-stack dry fit

- Assemble the actual pack in the Section 1 order with all rubbers, plugs, cable bends, hoses, A/C pipes and service tools represented.
- Confirm FS is centred on C0’s usable face and pushes rearward. Confirm FL pulls rearward and its complete shroud seals without core load.
- Prove removal paths for G0, FS, C0, R0 and FL; prove cap, drain, hose, drier, A/C union, plug and fastener access.
- Check clearances through engine movement and bonnet/latch closure. Nothing may rub a blade, belt, fin, pipe, wire or sharp edge.
- Photograph and record every released gap. Only now freeze hole coordinates and bracket drawings.

**Gate:** competent fabricator and project owner sign the dry-fit drawing before finish coating.

### Step 10 — Prepare and finish ferrous structure

1. Deburr and radius exposed edges; remove spatter without grinding structural welds flush.
2. Degrease, mechanically clean and use phosphoric rust treatment only on isolated ferrous steel where required. Keep it off aluminium, copper/brass, solder, rubbers and cores.
3. Apply a compatible 2K epoxy primer, then durable 2K polyurethane satin/semi-gloss black. Mask threads, earth points, mating faces, drains and rubber seats.
4. After full cure, reopen drains/threads and apply compatible cavity wax inside eligible closed steel sections. Keep wax off earths, rubbers, interfaces and heat-exchanger faces.
5. Install only new rubber and clean matching zinc hardware after coatings have cured.

**Safety:** 2K coatings may contain hazardous isocyanates. Use the product SDS, professional spray controls, correct supplied-air respiratory protection where required and local environmental rules.

### Step 11 — Plumbing, A/C and wiring

- Make the final neck map, then buy new moulded upper/lower radiator hoses and overflow hose. Use new constant-tension clamps aligned behind the hose beads.
- Fit the approved cap, Toyota 2H thermostat and seal, and one known compatible long-life coolant chemistry mixed with deionised water as its manufacturer specifies. Do not mix coolant families.
- Keep the new receiver-drier sealed until the A/C circuit is ready. Lubricate new HNBR O-rings only with the identified compatible refrigerant oil; evacuate, leak-check and recharge by the identified system specification through an A/C specialist.
- Run FS from A/C request through appropriate high-side-pressure control so it does not run blindly against abnormal pressure. Run FL from coolant-temperature control. Provide a safe service-test function.
- Each fan gets its own sealed relay, covered fuse, correctly sized cable, protected earth and voltage-drop/current record. Use sealed connectors, adhesive heat-shrink, black abrasion loom, grommets and rubber-lined metal P-clips; route exits rearward/downward with drip loops.
- Apply dielectric grease sparingly to connector seals only, never as a substitute for clean metal contact at terminals or earths.

### Step 12 — Commission and release

1. Cold leak-check coolant and A/C circuits; confirm caps, clamps, drains and fasteners.
2. Hand-turn both fans, then test each independently. Record fan-terminal voltage, start/run current, rotation, direction, relay/fuse/cable temperature and charging voltage.
3. Bleed coolant fully. Heat-cycle at idle with FL control verified; inspect for leaks, hose collapse, rub, vibration and saddle/top-mount movement.
4. Run A/C at hot idle in the intended ambient duty, historically up to 50 °C. Record ambient, refrigerant, high/low-side pressures, condenser inlet/outlet temperatures and centre-vent temperature. Acceptance limits come from the actual refrigerant/system specification, not this photo guide.
5. Road/load-test naturally aspirated cooling with ambient, speed/load, coolant temperature and fan state logged. Pass requires stable coolant without boil, purge or progressive rise and stable A/C performance without progressive high-side escalation.
6. If condenser performance fails, first correct fan direction, voltage drop, sealing, bypass and full-face airflow distribution. Add a shallow sealed plenum/duct only from a measured design. Do not add a second front fan without a new controlled revision.
7. Reinspect after cool-down and the first loaded journey. Recheck witness marks, leaks, rubber settling, hose strain, wiring, fasteners and fin contact.

- R0 original tanks, filler/cap neck, hose necks, rails, ears and lower locators; new core assumed in budget.

- G0 guard frame/mesh where sound.

## 7. Final acceptance checklist

- [ ] No turbo, K0/intercooler, charge pipe or second front fan remains in the package or purchase list.
- [ ] R0 passes written pressure/flow results; C0 passes specialist leak/cleanliness results.
- [ ] FS pushes engineward; FL pulls engineward; both have recorded electrical data and no abnormal rub/noise.
- [ ] Lower saddles carry all R0 weight; upper locators remain neutral; proof test passes.
- [ ] C0, FS, FL and G0 each have independent, isolated, removable supports; no through-core ties.
- [ ] Opaque dry fit and all service/removal paths pass before coating.
- [ ] Coatings are thin, crisp and absent from fins, seals, hose lands, earths, threads, labels, vents and rubbers.
- [ ] New drier, HNBR seals, hoses, clamps, thermostat/seal, approved cap and known coolant are fitted.
- [ ] Fan branches are independently fused/relayed and pass voltage-drop/current/temperature checks.
- [ ] Hot-idle A/C and naturally aspirated road/load cooling logs pass without progressive temperature or pressure rise.
- [ ] First heat-cycle and first-journey reinspection finds no leak, rub, settling or witness-mark movement.
