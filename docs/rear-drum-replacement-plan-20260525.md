# Rear Drum Replacement Plan

Date: 2026-05-25
Vehicle: 1978 Toyota Land Cruiser J40
Scope: both rear drum brake assemblies, rear parking-brake cable ends, rear axle hard lines, rear center flex hose, clips, and final bleed/adjustment.

## Goal

Replace the rear brakes as a matched left/right axle job. Do not rebuild one side only. The end state is:

- Both rear drums either proven serviceable and machined within limit, or both replaced.
- New rear shoes and full spring/hold-down/adjuster hardware.
- New wheel cylinders matched to the removed samples.
- New rear parking-brake cable assemblies and small cable hardware.
- New/recreated rear axle hard brake lines and rear center flex hose if disturbed or corroded.
- Fresh DOT 3 fluid, full bleed, leak test, shoe adjustment, and handbrake adjustment.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Do not open any hydraulic line until line caps/plugs, brake fluid, bleed hose/bottle, brake cleaner, rags, and catch tray are ready.
2. Do not discard old drums, shoes, wheel cylinders, cables, springs, clips, hard lines, or hose until the replacements physically match.
3. Do not buy rear shoes, wheel cylinders, drums, or cables from catalogue logic alone. Match old parts and measurements first.
4. Do not use generic rubber hose, plumbing fittings, bare copper pipe, single flares, or guessed flare nuts on brake hydraulics.
5. If one drum, cylinder, shoe set, cable, or critical spring is replaced, do the matching part on the other side too.
6. Final assembly, bleed, and road validation must be checked by someone competent with brake work.

## Parts And Consumables To Have Ready

Buy or prepare before teardown:

- Line caps/plugs for every opened brake port.
- Brake cleaner, rags, catch tray, nitrile gloves, eye protection.
- Clear bleed hose and catch bottle or bleeder.
- Fresh sealed DOT 3 brake fluid.
- Labels, marker, bags, tape measure, caliper, and camera.
- Brake spring/hold-down tool, line wrenches, normal metric sockets/spanners, torque wrench.

Parts to release only after measurement/sample match:

- Rear brake shoes, one axle set.
- Rear spring/hold-down/adjuster hardware kit.
- Rear wheel cylinders, actual count confirmed by teardown.
- Wheel-cylinder mounting bolts/washers if old ones are rusty.
- Rear drums, qty 2 only if measured bad, cracked, deeply scored, stepped, out-of-round, or unsafe to machine.
- Rear parking-brake cable set with clevises, clips, adjusters, return springs, and retainers.
- Rear center flex hose, complete crimped brake hose assembly only.
- Rear axle hard brake lines in brake-rated 4.75 mm / 3/16 in tube, with matching flare nuts and correct double/inverted flare only after the removed samples confirm seat/thread.
- Brake-line clips, cable retainers, rubber sleeves/grommets, and rubber-lined P-clips where original support is missing.

## Stage 1 - Capture Before Removal

Do this before a spring, cable, or pipe is removed.

1. Photograph both rear brakes with the drums still fitted.
2. Photograph the cable route, backing-plate lever, return spring, clevis/adjuster, and cable bracket on both sides.
3. Photograph the rear center flex hose, axle T/union, hard-line route, and every clip position.
4. Remove the drums only after the vehicle is safely supported.
5. Photograph each opened drum before touching the springs or shoes.
6. Label the photos and parts as left rear and right rear.

Required measurements:

- Drum inside diameter on both drums.
- Maximum drum diameter from the drum stamping or workshop manual.
- Shoe width and lining arc/length.
- Wheel-cylinder count per side.
- Wheel-cylinder bore if readable/measurable.
- Wheel-cylinder mounting bolt spacing.
- Wheel-cylinder port thread and bleed screw position.
- Parking-brake cable overall length, sheath length, and both end styles.
- Hard-line tube OD, fitting thread/seat, route length, and clip positions.

## Stage 2 - Strip Down Safely

Work one side at a time and keep the opposite side assembled as a reference until the first side is fully documented.

1. Back off shoe adjusters if the drum catches on a wear lip.
2. Remove drum and photograph inside again if anything was hidden.
3. Remove return springs, hold-downs, adjusters, shoes, and parking-brake lever parts.
4. Bag and label old hardware by side and location.
5. Disconnect parking-brake cable at the backing plate and equalizer/intermediate link. Keep the cable intact if possible.
6. Disconnect hydraulic hard line only when caps/plugs are ready. Cap the open port immediately.
7. Remove wheel cylinders and keep them as purchase samples.
8. Remove hard lines intact where possible so they remain bend templates.

Stop if a fitting rounds, a line twists with the nut, or a cylinder bolt is seized. At that point, decide whether the line, hose, cylinder, or backing plate must be sacrificed and replaced.

## Stage 3 - Inspect

Rear drums:

- Replace both drums if either drum is cracked, heat-checked badly, deeply scored, heavily stepped, out-of-round, or at/over service limit.
- Machine only if the finished diameter will remain below the maximum service diameter and the machine shop can cut both evenly.
- If one drum is not safely usable, replace both drums as a pair.

Shoes and friction surfaces:

- Replace shoes as an axle set.
- If shoes are oil-soaked, fix the axle seal or wheel-cylinder leak before installing new shoes.
- Do not clean and reuse contaminated friction material.

Wheel cylinders:

- Replace cylinders as a matched rear set unless a specialist confirms rebuild is better and bores are clean.
- Bleed screws must open cleanly and point where bleeding is possible.

Backing plates:

- Reuse only if not cracked, bent, or deeply grooved at shoe contact pads.
- Lightly dress shoe pads if needed, then apply only a tiny amount of high-temp brake grease on contact pads and adjuster threads.
- Never grease shoe lining or drum friction surface.

Parking brake:

- Replace rear cables as new assemblies.
- Keep old cables for length, sheath, end fittings, bracket positions, clevis, adjuster, and spring reference.
- Confirm both rear levers move freely and return fully.

Hydraulic lines:

- Replace/recreate rear axle hard lines if corroded, kinked, seized, unsupported, or disturbed.
- Replace rear center flex hose if old, cracked, unknown, stretched, or if fittings are disturbed.
- Route lines clear of tyres, springs, shocks, U-bolts, exhaust, and axle travel.

## Stage 4 - Order By Samples

Take these old parts to the supplier or brake specialist:

- Both rear wheel cylinders.
- One shoe from each side.
- Springs, hold-down pins/cups, adjusters, and parking-brake lever hardware.
- Parking-brake cables with both ends intact.
- Rear center flex hose.
- Old rear axle hard lines and flare nuts.
- One sample of each brake-line clip and cable retainer.

Supplier instruction:

- Match the old parts physically before payment.
- For drums, use the measured inside diameter and service-limit decision. Do not import or buy heavy drums unless local machining/replacement is not acceptable.
- For hard lines, use brake-rated 4.75 mm / 3/16 in tube and matching flare nuts. Confirm flare type and thread from the removed samples.
- For flex hose, use only a complete crimped brake hose assembly rated for brake use.

## Stage 5 - Reassemble

1. Clean backing plates and mounting faces.
2. Fit new wheel cylinders with correct bolts/washers and correct port/bleeder orientation.
3. Fit recreated hard lines without twisting or cross-threading flare nuts.
4. Fit shoes, adjusters, hold-downs, and return springs exactly as the documented side shows.
5. Fit parking-brake lever/cable parts and confirm free movement before the drum goes on.
6. Fit drums after cleaning friction surfaces with brake cleaner.
7. Route cables and lines through proper retainers. Add rubber-lined P-clips where original support is missing.
8. Torque disturbed fasteners to the workshop manual/spec used by the mechanic.

## Stage 6 - Adjust And Bleed

1. Adjust rear shoes first, before adjusting the handbrake cable.
2. Set each drum so it turns freely with light, even shoe contact only as specified by the workshop method.
3. Adjust parking brake so both rear wheels hold evenly and release fully.
4. Bleed the brake system with fresh DOT 3 until fluid is clean and no air returns.
5. Hold firm pedal pressure and inspect every cylinder, flare nut, hose end, T/union, and bleeder for leaks.
6. Spin both rear wheels and confirm no brake drag after release.

## Stage 7 - Validation

Before road use:

- Pedal is firm and does not sink under steady pressure.
- No fluid leak at any connection or wheel cylinder boot.
- Both drums rotate freely after brake release.
- Parking brake holds both wheels and releases completely.
- Brake pedal returns properly.
- No hose, hard line, parking-brake cable, or clip touches moving suspension, tyre, spring, shock, or U-bolt.

Road validation:

1. Low-speed yard test only first.
2. Stop straight from walking speed.
3. Recheck leaks and drum temperature by hand-near test, not by grabbing a hot drum.
4. Short road test only after the yard test passes.
5. Recheck shoe adjustment, handbrake adjustment, leaks, and fasteners after bedding.
6. Recheck again after 50 km and 500 km.

## Stop And Ask Before Continuing

Stop the job and get approval before proceeding if any of these appear:

- Drum is over service limit or the service limit cannot be confirmed.
- One side is a different shoe width, cylinder layout, or spring layout than expected.
- Axle seal leak or gear oil contamination is found.
- Backing plate is cracked, bent, or badly grooved.
- Wheel-cylinder mounting holes or ports do not match available local parts.
- Brake hard-line flare/thread/seat cannot be confirmed.
- Supplier offers generic hose, plumbing fittings, bare copper, or non-brake-rated line.
- Pedal will not become firm after bleeding.
- Parking brake holds unevenly or does not release fully.
