# Toyota 2H Low-Mount Turbo — Controlled Build Process

## Released Package Direction

Plan on the recommended hardware being available:

- Toyota `2H`-specific **low-mount** exhaust manifold;
- `CT26` four-bolt turbo mounting pattern;
- compact `TD05H 16G` turbo with `7 cm² / .49 A/R` turbine housing;
- internal mechanical wastegate;
- `5-7 psi` initial boost calibration;
- intercooling and pre-turbine EGT, boost, oil-pressure and coolant-temperature monitoring.

This releases a package direction, not blind bolt-in fit. The manifold-to-head and turbo-to-manifold interfaces have strong documented fit support. Steering, bonnet, wing, air-cleaner, A/C, downpipe, oil-drain, heat and service clearances remain physical verification points on this converted J40.

## Non-Negotiable Build Rules

1. Do not alter fuelling before the complete air, oil, exhaust, cooling and monitoring systems pass static tests.
2. Do not fabricate the hot side until the final J60 hydraulic-steering envelope is fitted or hard-mocked.
3. Do not weld the sump in place. Remove it, weld the drain bung above normal oil level, clean it completely and refit it with a verified seal.
4. Do not support the exhaust from the turbo or manifold. Use independent brackets/hangers and flex provision.
5. Do not cut or bulge the bonnet as the first packaging solution. Keep the turbo low, relocate the air cleaner, re-clock housings and revise pipe routes first.
6. Do not begin at `8-10 psi`. Commission at `5-7 psi`; higher boost requires logged evidence and a new release decision.

## Stage 1 — Identify and Record the Baseline

1. Photograph the 2H engine number/code, injection-pump tag, injector type, manifold face, oil-filter housing, vacuum-pump arrangement, sump and both sides of the fitted engine.
2. Confirm the engine is on its final mounts and record mount condition.
3. Record the installed steering, A/C compressor and hose envelope, starter, alternator, battery, brake/clutch hydraulics, fuel lines and wiring routes.
4. Photograph the bonnet inner reinforcement and wing geometry with the engine bay assembled.
5. At standard fuelling, record hot idle and loaded coolant temperature, oil pressure, smoke behaviour and any oil/coolant consumption.

**Gate G1A:** No package fabrication until identity and the as-fitted baseline are recorded.

## Stage 2 — Pass the Engine-Health Gate

1. Warm the engine fully and record compression for all six cylinders with one gauge and one procedure.
2. If any cylinder or spread is questionable, diagnose with leak-down or the appropriate targeted test.
3. Measure oil pressure fully hot at idle and Toyota's specified test rpm.
4. Measure warm crankcase pressure/blow-by; do not rely only on an oil-cap impression.
5. Pressure-test the cooling system and cap; verify thermostat, pump, radiator flow, fan/shroud and loaded temperature stability.
6. Test injectors for opening pressure, pattern, leakage and balance. Have the pump assessed by a diesel specialist.
7. Stop for a weak cylinder, low hot oil pressure, heavy blow-by, overheating, coolant pressurisation, injector dribble, uncontrolled fuelling or serious baseline smoke.

**Gate G1:** A signed engine-health sheet is required before the turbo is installed.

## Stage 3 — Inspect and Freeze the Supplied Package

At goods receipt, confirm rather than infer:

- manifold is specifically for the Toyota 2H head and is the low-mount version;
- turbo inlet and manifold use the same CT26 four-bolt pattern;
- turbo is TD05H with the ordered 16G compressor specification;
- turbine housing is marked/documented as `7 cm²` or approximately `.49 A/R`;
- wastegate is internal, moves freely and has a documented base pressure compatible with the `5-7 psi` start target;
- compressor and turbine housings can be clocked without compromising centre-housing orientation or actuator operation;
- oil feed/drain threads and coolant-port requirements are identified;
- turbine outlet, gaskets, studs and downpipe interface are physically measured;
- serial numbers, tags and seller documentation are photographed before installation.

**Gate G2:** Reject or hold any unit whose identity, wheel/housing specification, actuator or flange differs from the order.

## Stage 4 — Establish Fixed Vehicle Datums

1. Fit or hard-mock the final J60 steering box, column/shaft, pump, brackets and pressure/return hose envelopes.
2. Fit the engine and gearbox on final mounts.
3. Refit the relevant wing panels and bonnet with the real hinges, catches and bump rubbers.
4. Mark the bonnet reinforcement, steering, engine-movement and service-tool envelopes.
5. Record the current round air-cleaner location; plan for its relocation or replacement with a compact sealed remote air cleaner.

**Gate G3A:** Steering and body datums control the turbo position. Do not design around absent components.

## Stage 5 — Mock the Complete Hot Side

1. Trial-fit the manifold to the bare, cleaned head face using temporary fastening suitable for mock-up.
2. Fit the turbo, centre housing, wastegate actuator and compressor inlet/outlet elbows.
3. Keep the turbo oil-drain axis within the turbo manufacturer's permitted orientation.
4. Add a rigid mock-up of the first downpipe bend, planned service joint, flex provision and final heat-shield thickness.
5. Support the turbo/downpipe mock-up so no bending load is placed on the manifold.
6. Check steering, starter, oil-filter removal, engine mount, A/C, brake/clutch/fuel routes, wiring and hand/tool access.
7. Mark the turbine/downpipe heat envelope and the engine's movement envelope separately.

**Gate G3:** The selected position must preserve steering function, a continuous gravity oil-drain path, service access and safe heat separation.

## Stage 6 — Prove Bonnet and Wing Clearance

1. Fit the actual actuator, inlet elbow, charge outlet and representative heat shield—not only the bare turbo.
2. Place clay cones at the highest points and on nearby bonnet reinforcement.
3. Close the bonnet gently to the proper latch position; never force it against the mock-up.
4. Reopen and measure the compressed clay. Target approximately `25 mm` minimum static clearance after the final heat shield is included, with additional allowance for engine movement.
5. Rock or jack the engine only within the safe travel of its mounts and confirm no possible contact.
6. Photograph every clay point and record the minimum value.
7. If clearance fails, in order: re-clock the compressor; revise elbows/pipe routing; lower or revise the mount/manifold solution; change the remote-air-cleaner location. Bonnet alteration is a last-resort redesign requiring separate approval.

**Gate G4:** No permanent hot-side fabrication unless the bonnet closes with recorded clearance and engine-roll allowance.

## Stage 7 — Build the Oil and Conditional Coolant Circuits

1. Identify and measure a suitable engine oil-gallery feed point and hot pressure.
2. Follow the exact turbo supplier's feed-line and restrictor requirement. Do not fit a universal restrictor by guesswork.
3. Route the feed away from exhaust heat, abrasion and unsupported heavy fittings.
4. Use a large-bore drain from the centre housing with continuous downward fall, no trap, kink, uphill run or submerged sump entry.
5. Remove the sump. Position the drain bung above the normal oil level, weld it, clean the sump internally and inspect for debris before refitting.
6. If the supplied centre housing requires water cooling, use approved engine take-off and return points, preserve heater/cooling function and avoid air traps. If it does not require water cooling, do not add unnecessary circuits.

**Gate G5A:** Demonstrate free drain flow and pressure-tight feed/coolant circuits before first start.

## Stage 8 — Fabricate Exhaust and Heat Protection

1. Build a smooth, adequately sized first downpipe bend and serviceable turbine-outlet joint.
2. Provide flex provision after a correctly supported rigid section.
3. Use independent brackets and chassis/body hangers so the turbo and manifold do not carry exhaust mass.
4. Complete a low-restriction single exhaust with safe body, floor, propshaft and line clearance.
5. Fabricate removable air-gap shields around the manifold, turbine and downpipe.
6. Protect steering, brake/clutch hydraulics, fuel, wiring, A/C, intake ducting, bonnet insulation and paint.
7. Retain access to manifold/turbo fasteners and inspection points.

## Stage 9 — Build Intake, Intercooler and Breather Routes

1. Relocate or replace the large round air cleaner with a sealed, serviceable remote unit sized for the engine.
2. Use collapse-resistant clean-air ducting with supported joints and no unfiltered connection.
3. Build the charge route using `2.0-2.5 in` as the working range, finalized from supplied outlets and pressure-loss requirements.
4. Use beaded pipe ends, reinforced couplers, correctly sized constant-tension/T-bolt clamps and independent supports.
5. Provide flexible sections for engine movement without allowing pipes to strike the bonnet, wing, steering or cooling pack.
6. Pressure-test the complete compressor-to-manifold route.
7. Route crankcase ventilation through a low-restriction serviceable separator into the clean-air duct before the compressor, based on measured blow-by.

## Stage 10 — Install Monitoring and Protection

Install before any fuelling change:

- calibrated pre-turbine EGT probe and gauge/alarm;
- low-range boost gauge or sensor;
- calibrated engine oil-pressure gauge/alarm while retaining the factory warning where practical;
- calibrated coolant-temperature gauge/alarm while retaining the factory warning where practical.

Use a fused ignition-switched branch, clean grounds, protected sender wiring, correct thermocouple extension wire and labelled serviceable connectors. Bench-test gauge response, alarms, illumination and key-off behaviour.

**Gate G5B:** No loaded tuning until every instrument and alarm has been function-tested.

## Stage 11 — Prime, Leak-Test and Heat-Cycle

1. Refill with the approved engine oil/filter and coolant.
2. Keep pump fuelling at the baseline setting.
3. Disable starting and crank until oil reaches the turbo and drains freely to the sump; follow the turbo supplier's priming method.
4. Start and idle without load. Inspect oil feed/drain, coolant, exhaust and intake joints.
5. Pressure-test the charge system and verify wastegate linkage/reference hose.
6. Complete several controlled heat cycles, monitoring EGT, oil pressure and coolant temperature.
7. Inspect fasteners and witness marks; re-torque only to the hardware/manifold specification.
8. Recheck bonnet/wing clearance, heat shields and all flexible routes after thermal movement.

**Gate G5:** Static integrity, monitoring and heat-cycle checks must all pass before road load.

## Stage 12 — Commission at 5–7 PSI

1. Verify the mechanical wastegate limits boost to the initial `5-7 psi` range.
2. Begin with short, low-load runs and progress load in defined steps.
3. Log engine speed/load condition, boost, pre-turbine EGT, coolant temperature, hot oil pressure and visible smoke at each step.
4. Stop for unstable/creeping boost, excessive smoke, EGT or coolant rise, oil-pressure loss, leakage, abnormal noise, pipe/body contact or clutch slip.
5. Reinspect and heat-cycle after the first loaded run and again after the initial road-validation period.
6. Permit small diesel-specialist pump adjustments only after baseline logs are stable and the charge system is leak-free.
7. Do not approve `8-10 psi` from seat-of-the-pants impressions. It requires clean logged thermal/lubrication/smoke results, acceptable exhaust drive pressure where measured, and clutch/driveline acceptance.

**Gate G6:** Road release is limited to the proven configuration and recorded `5-7 psi` calibration.

## Minimum Handover Record

The workshop handover pack must contain:

- engine-health readings and test conditions;
- manifold and turbo identification photographs;
- flange/port/wastegate measurements;
- bonnet clay-clearance photographs and minimum measurement;
- oil-feed source and drain-path photographs;
- exhaust supports and heat-shield photographs;
- charge-system pressure-test result;
- gauge/alarm function record;
- first-start and heat-cycle inspection sheets;
- staged road-test logs and the final wastegate/fuelling settings.
