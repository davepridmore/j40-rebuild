# J40 retained original radiator and full-face condenser cooling pack — Rev K

**Issue:** 2 August 2026<br>
**Vehicle/duty:** Toyota J40, Toyota 2H turbo plan, 150 bhp maximum, 50 °C ambient acceptance<br>
**Status:** packaging concept and measurement-controlled shop plan; not yet a fit or thermal release<br>
**Supersedes:** Rev J, whose narrow side-by-side condenser was not the retained project part

## 1. Start here — what the shop is actually building

Use the two real retained heat exchangers shown in the project photographs:

1. **R0 — the original wide copper/brass engine radiator.** This remains the only engine-coolant radiator.
2. **C0 — the original near-full-face A/C condenser, including its existing pipes, receiver/drier and mounting tabs.** Keep it full size if it passes pressure, flush, fin and fit checks.

Add only one new heat exchanger:

3. **K0 — one shallow turbo charge-air cooler (CAC/intercooler).** It is not another water radiator.

There are exactly three installed fans:

- two matching Toyota/Denso-style electric **pushers** at the front, level and horizontally centred as one group on the vehicle centreline; and
- the retained large 2H mechanical **puller** behind R0, moving air toward the engine.

The corrected front-to-rear arrangement is:

**stone guard → two matching centred front fans → shallow low-centred K0 → retained full-face C0 → retained full-face R0 → sealed rear shroud → retained large mechanical fan → engine**

K0 overlaps only the lower central part of C0. The upper C0/R0 face remains a two-core path. This is the smallest simple arrangement that keeps both real retained parts and avoids a side tower. It is a **provisional packaging position** until the complete depth, bumper/grille opening, latch, pipes and service access are measured on the vehicle.

## 2. What changed from Rev J

Rev J illustrated an invented narrow condenser beside the CAC. The retained condenser photograph proves that this is wrong: C0 is a broad, near-full-face unit with its own side pipework, receiver/drier and tabs. Rev K therefore:

- preserves the actual C0 shape and fittings;
- preserves the actual R0 tanks, necks, filler, rails, ears and lower locator geometry;
- places C0 in its normal full-face layer directly in front of R0;
- makes K0 the only additional core;
- uses a compact low-front K0 position rather than adding width at either side; and
- keeps all renders subordinate to the physical measurement and test gates below.

Do not fabricate, purchase or quote from any Rev J side-by-side-core image.

### K0 starting target — not a released purchase dimension

Use **approximately 450–500 mm core width × 150–180 mm core height × no more than about 65 mm core depth** only as a first mock-up box. The complete tanks, ports, couplers and brackets will be larger. Change the target to suit measured `W0/H0/P0-lower`; do not force the vehicle or retained C0 around a catalogue intercooler.

### Required depth calculations

Record actual complete-part depths and gaps on the 1:1 fixture:

- upper zone: `P-required-upper = guard + fan carrier + clearance + C0 + 10–15 gap + R0 + rear shroud/fan clearance`;
- lower K0-overlap zone: `P-required-lower = P-required-upper + complete K0 depth + its two anti-contact/service gaps`.

PASS only when both values are less than their measured available depths with bonnet/latch, bumper, grille, pipes, hoses, wiring, service tools and engine movement represented. Do not add the nominal figures on paper and declare fit.

## 5. Front-fan position — visibly central and symmetric

Put F1 and F2 side-by-side at the same height on one removable carrier. Let:

- `Wc` = usable fan-carrier width;
- `F` = outside width of one complete matching module;
- `g` = clear gap between complete modules; use 10–15 mm preferred; and
- `M` = equal left/right outer margin.

Use:

`M = (Wc − 2F − g) / 2`

Release only if:

- `M > 0`;
- left and right margins match within 2 mm;
- the fan-pair midpoint is on vehicle centreline within 2 mm;
- fan hubs are level within 2 mm;
- both units push air from grille to engine;
- the complete pair delivers at least **3,000 m³/h installed at 13.5 V**, with **3,300 m³/h or more preferred**, through the finished guard/cores; and
- plug, cable, blade, guard, tab and removal clearances are proven.

Free-air catalogue CFM is not an installed-airflow test.

## 6. Mounting — a continuous lower load cradle plus both intended top holes

R0 is the largest and heaviest core when filled. It must not appear to float between the uprights and it must not hang from C0, K0, the stone guard or either fan carrier. Its primary load path is:

**R0 lower locators → two Toyota-pattern rubber saddles → full-width lower crossmember → gusseted end connections → both structural side uprights → chassis/front support.**

For a measured clear crossmember span no greater than 700 mm and a completely filled R0 mass no greater than 35 kg, start the fixture with one straight **40 × 40 × 3 mm mild-steel SHS** lower crossmember. Provide a flat saddle seat above it at each measured `S0` location and a drain/open path below. Use two 4 mm triangular gussets at each crossmember/upright junction, approximately 50 mm legs, or an equivalently stiff removable end-plate connection. This is a fabrication starting minimum, not permission to cut: if the actual span/mass exceeds either limit, or the attachment is offset/twisted, recalculate and release the section and connections before manufacture.

Each rubber saddle must fully capture its R0 lower pin/rail while leaving the tank, soldered seam and fins clear of steel. The two saddles must share the load without twisting R0; shim only beneath the removable saddle seat, never by forcing the radiator.

R0 must also visibly and physically attach to both existing upper horizontal return plates. These upper connections resist fore/aft and sideways movement; they do not carry the filled radiator's vertical weight.

At each side:

1. position the short removable black radiator ear directly below the existing hole in the inward-facing grey horizontal top plate;
2. place a large washer and vertical sleeved bolt above that exact hole; select bolt diameter only after measuring the hole and complete bush stack;
3. pass the bolt down through the plate, a sleeved EPDM isolator and the radiator ear;
4. retain it with washer and locking nut below, without crushing the rubber or pulling the radiator sideways; and
5. support R0's weight on two lower rubber saddles.

Both top holes must be occupied. Do not substitute a side-face bolt, new chassis hole, slot, reaming, forced alignment or a visual air gap between the return/isolator/ear stack. C0, K0, fans and electrical boxes each use independent removable brackets; no heat exchanger carries another component.

Before paint, weigh R0 filled and capped, then proof-load the empty cradle vertically to **2 × `MR`** for 10 minutes using a distributed dummy load at the two saddle centres. PASS requires no permanent set, cracked weld, loosened fastener, saddle displacement or upright distortion. Then install R0 and perform a fore/aft/side restraint shake check without metal-to-tank contact.

## 7. Air sealing and serviceability

- Seal fan-carrier edges so air cannot bypass the C0/R0 active face.
- Use a shallow removable lower K0 cradle with rubber isolation and a local duct/seal; do not cable-tie through fins.
- Keep at least 10 mm anti-contact spacing between separate cores unless the 1:1 fixture proves a larger service gap is required.
- C0 and R0 remain separately removable without cutting pipes, brackets or the chassis.
- K0 and its charge pipes remain removable without discharging the A/C circuit.
- Keep radiator cap, drain, hoses, A/C fittings/drier, fan plugs, belts and covered electrical lids accessible.
- Keep the covered relay/MIDI carrier inside the existing front-view silhouette, preferably on the structural carrier above or inboard of the core edge; never add a side tower.

## 8. Electrical control

Each electric fan has its own correctly sized relay, covered MIDI/fuse branch, cable, connector and earth based on measured start/run current and voltage drop.

Normal staged control may use A/C pressure/demand, coolant temperature, boost/high IAT and manual emergency override. High coolant temperature, high IAT, high A/C pressure, high load or sensor/control fault commands both front fans. The large rear mechanical fan remains engine-driven and always pulls toward the engine.

## 9. 50 °C / turbo release — performance must be demonstrated

The layout is intended for 50 °C ambient and the 150 bhp turbo plan, but no image can prove it is comfortable at that duty. Release requires:

- R0 heat rejection target: at least **115 kW continuous** and **130 kW for 10 minutes** at the defined acceptance duty;
- post-CAC IAT no higher than **80 °C** at the agreed boost/load point;
- complete charge-route pressure drop no higher than **10 kPa** at that point;
- no progressive coolant, IAT or A/C-pressure rise at hot idle with A/C operating;
- no boost/load derate caused by cooling inside the accepted 150 bhp envelope; and
- safe currents, voltage drop, cable/fuse/relay temperature, vibration and clearances.

Instrument ambient, coolant inlet/outlet, pre/post-CAC IAT, boost, EGT, A/C high-side pressure, fan commands, system voltage and each fan current. Use a controlled 50 °C cell or documented equivalent heat-soak/load simulation followed by the hottest available road/load test.

If the lower triple-overlap zone causes progressive coolant temperature, excessive A/C head pressure or excessive IAT, **stop**. Do not shrink C0, remove A/C protection or increase boost. First improve sealing/ducting and confirm fan/radiator condition; if it still fails, relocate K0 to an isolated-airflow position or change to a separately engineered water-to-air charge-cooling system under a new revision.

## 10. Fabricator sequence

1. Put actual R0, actual C0, guard, fan/shroud and vehicle in the shop together.
2. Record every row in the dimensional transfer sheet and make rigid `B0` top-hole and `S0` lower-saddle templates.
3. Clean/test R0 and C0; stop if either retained part fails.
4. Build a rigid full-size dummy for the two matching fan modules and proposed K0 complete envelope.
5. Assemble an opaque 1:1 front-to-rear fixture with bumper, grille, latch, pipes, hoses, wiring, covers and service tools represented.
6. Prove the continuous lower-crossmember load path, both saddle centres, both intended top holes, rear fan sweep and every removal path; complete the `2 × MR` cradle proof load.
7. Only then buy K0/fan modules and make removable carriers, ears, seals and covered electrical brackets.
8. Pressure/flow/electrical test, install-airflow test, then instrument the 50 °C duty.
9. Record evidence and owner sign-off before paint/final finish.
