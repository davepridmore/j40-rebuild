# Rear drum brake reconstruction plan — 2026-07-16

## Corrected identification

The larger new circular parts are the **rear brake drums**, not front discs. They may be the correct drums and the smaller old drums may have been incorrect, but this must be proved by the fitted geometry. Do not alter the new drums to imitate the old ones.

The rusty circular carrier is the rear brake backing plate/shoe carrier assembly. The centre section visible in the assembled photo is the axle hub/flange and wheel-stud area; it is not itself a brake disc.

## Required setup

- Rated stands under the chassis, wheels chocked, axle separately supported, and no hose or cable carrying axle weight.
- Work on **one side only at first**. Keep the opposite side assembled as the orientation reference.
- Label trays `LH` and `RH`; never mix adjusters, parking-brake levers, pins, springs or wheel-cylinder pieces.
- Before opening hydraulics: have line caps/plugs, catch tray, clear bleed hose, fresh DOT 3, brake cleaner, lint-free cloths and eye/hand protection ready.
- Use proper brake-spring pliers and hold-down tools. Do not grip or stretch springs with random locking pliers.

## Phase 1 — Record and measure before cleaning

1. Photograph each side square-on, from the top, and from the rear of the backing plate. Include the hard-line entry, bleed screw, parking-brake cable, adjuster orientation and every spring hook.
2. Mark drum, backing plate, shoes, adjuster and parking-brake lever `LH` or `RH`.
3. Record the new and old drum dimensions side by side:
   - internal braking diameter;
   - internal braking depth and overall height/offset;
   - centre register/bore;
   - wheel-stud/bolt-hole count, PCD and hole diameter;
   - mounting-face position;
   - usable shoe-track width;
   - any maximum-diameter marking.
4. Record shoe width, arc, web-hole positions, lining thickness and parking-brake/adjuster contact features.
5. Measure remade versus reference springs: free length, wire diameter, coil outside diameter/count, end/hook shape and hook clocking. Measure hold-down pin length, shaft diameter, head size and cup engagement.

### Drum acceptance gate

The larger new drum may proceed only if it seats fully and concentrically on the correct register, the mounting face sits flush, the shoe track covers the full lining width, the backing plate and hardware fit within its depth, and the drum rotates without touching the backing plate, wheel cylinder, springs, pins, adjuster or parking-brake lever. A larger outside diameter alone is not a fault.

## Phase 2 — Strip without losing orientation

1. Back off the adjuster fully and release the parking brake before removing the drum.
2. Photograph the exposed assembly again before removing any spring.
3. Remove the shoe return springs with the proper tool, then hold-down cups/springs/pins, adjuster/strut and shoes. Note which shoe carries the parking-brake lever.
4. Disconnect the parking-brake cable/lever without losing its clevis, clip, washer or return spring.
5. If replacing the wheel cylinder, clean the pipe union, use a flare-nut spanner, cap the line immediately and remove the cylinder without twisting the hard line.
6. Bag every removed item by side and location. Old parts remain patterns until the rebuilt axle passes testing.

## Phase 3 — Clean and inspect

### Backing plate and hub area

1. Vacuum/brush loose debris; use brake cleaner for brake residue. Do not blow dust with compressed air.
2. Remove loose rust from the backing plate with hand tools or a controlled small wire brush. Do not thin the plate, enlarge holes or grind shoe contact pads.
3. Inspect for cracks, perforation, distortion, loose mounting, elongated pin/cylinder holes, grooved shoe pads, seized pivots and damaged cable anchors.
4. Check behind the plate for axle-seal or hub-seal leakage. Any oil on the brake side stops assembly until the seal fault is repaired.
5. Protect only the exterior/non-contact steel. No paint, converter, grease or undercoat on the drum track, shoe linings, shoe contact pads, cylinder mounts/ports, bleed screw, threads, flare seats, adjuster teeth or rubber parts.

### Drums

1. Degrease new drums completely, including preservative on the internal braking track.
2. Inspect both new and old drums for cracks, scoring, heat checks, pitting, damaged registers and distorted mounting faces.
3. Measure internal diameter at several clock positions and depths; record taper and out-of-round. Use the correct Toyota/manual or drum-stamped maximum diameter—do not invent a service limit from the old drum.
4. Clean the hub/drum mounting faces to bare, flat metal. A rust scale trapped here will create runout.

### Reusable small parts

- Adjuster: threads clean, correct handing, no stripped teeth, turns freely with no excessive play.
- Parking-brake lever/pivot: no oval hole, heavy groove, crack or binding.
- Used pins: correct length/diameter, straight, no necking or deep wear groove, sound head, full cup retention. Replace on any doubt.
- Remade springs: correct position-specific dimensions and hooks, consistent side-to-side, no coil bind, permanent set or rubbing. Because spring rate cannot be proved from appearance, a brake specialist should confirm the copied material/rate; use the Centric kit pieces wherever they correctly match.
- Shoes: new and dry, no grease/oil/brake-fluid contamination, correct leading/trailing or primary/secondary placement where the design distinguishes them.

## Phase 4 — Dry-build the first side

1. Lightly lubricate only approved metal-to-metal points with high-temperature brake lubricant: the backing-plate shoe pads, adjuster threads/contact ends and parking-brake lever pivot. Use a smear, not excess.
2. Fit the confirmed wheel cylinder with correct fasteners. Orient the bleed screw so air can escape at the top and confirm the pipe port/seat matches before tightening the hard line.
3. Transfer/install the parking-brake lever and its new/verified retaining clip on the correct shoe.
4. Position shoes against the wheel-cylinder ends and lower anchor/adjuster exactly as the retained side/reference photographs show.
5. Install hold-down pins from behind, then cups and springs. Confirm each cup positively locks and each pin remains centred.
6. Install the adjuster/strut in the correct direction and handing. Confirm its operating lever advances—not retracts—the adjuster in service.
7. Install lower and upper return springs in their recorded holes with correct hook direction. No spring may rub the hub, cylinder boot or drum.
8. Operate the shoes by hand: both move squarely and snap fully back; the adjuster remains seated; the parking-brake lever applies and fully returns.
9. Offer the larger new drum without force. Rotate it through 360 degrees. If it will not pass over the shoes, first confirm the adjuster is fully retracted and every shoe/anchor is seated—do not grind the shoes or drum to force fit.
10. Temporarily secure the drum to the hub with wheel nuts and spacers/washers fitted backwards only if the workshop procedure permits. Check rotation, seating and runout using the specified Toyota/manual method.

Do not dismantle the untouched reference side until the first side has passed dry-build inspection and its final layout has been photographed.

## Phase 5 — Rebuild the second side

Repeat Phases 1–4 for the other side. Mirror only the features that are genuinely handed. Confirm:

- wheel cylinders are in their correct LH/RH or position;
- adjusters are handed correctly and both advance in service;
- shoe/parking-brake lever placement matches the system design;
- spring holes and hook directions match the side-specific reference;
- both drums seat to the same mounting relationship and rotate freely.

## Phase 6 — Lines, parking brake and adjustment

1. Reproduce rear axle hard lines in brake-rated `4.75 mm / 3/16 in` tube using the old routes and confirmed matching steel flare nuts/seats. Make at least five clean practice double/inverted flares before making a vehicle line.
2. Fit the new/sample-matched rear centre hose. At supported droop and normal ride position it must not stretch, twist or contact the tyre, exhaust, shock, spring or axle.
3. Refit original-location clips, rubber sleeves/grommets and supports. No cable ties, compression fittings or unsupported spans.
4. Fit the locally copied parking-brake splitter/equalizer and all verified clevises, pins, clips and new springs.
5. With the parking-brake cable initially slack, adjust the shoes through the drum adjuster until slight even drag is felt, then back off to the vehicle/manual setting so both drums turn freely and similarly.
6. Only after shoe adjustment, set parking-brake cable free play. Confirm equal pull at both wheels and full release with the axle at normal height and droop.

## Phase 7 — Hydraulic and functional release

1. Fill only with fresh sealed DOT 3. Bleed in the Toyota/manual wheel sequence appropriate to the fitted hydraulic layout; do not assume a generic sequence if the circuit routing differs.
2. Check every cylinder boot, bleed screw, flare union, hose end and T/union for leakage during firm sustained pedal pressure.
3. Confirm a firm pedal, both rear wheels applying, both releasing without drag, and no hard-line/hose/cable contact through axle travel.
4. Refit wheels and torque wheel fasteners to the correct Toyota/wheel specification in stages.
5. Conduct a low-speed controlled brake test before public-road use: straight stop, no pull, no noise, no overheating and parking brake holds/releases evenly.
6. Bed the new shoes progressively. Recheck drum temperature side-to-side after short stops; a markedly hotter side means drag or uneven adjustment.
7. Reinspect for leaks, loose fasteners and adjustment after the first test, again after bedding/approximately 50 km, and at the project’s 500 km brake/suspension recheck.

## Hard stop conditions

Stop and repair/replace before assembly if any of these are found:

- drum does not seat concentrically/flush or exceeds its marked/manual diameter limit;
- deep drum pitting on the shoe track, crack, heat checking or excessive runout;
- backing-plate perforation, crack, distortion, loose mounting or badly grooved shoe pads;
- axle/hub seal leakage onto the brake side;
- contaminated shoe lining;
- incorrect wheel-cylinder bore, port, mounting, pushrod or bleed orientation;
- worn/bent used pin, insecure cup, mismatched/remade spring of uncertain material or rate;
- adjuster fitted to the wrong side or operating backwards;
- any spring, pin, shoe or lever touching the drum/hub;
- hydraulic leak, weak pedal, unequal braking, failure to return or parking-brake imbalance.

## Completion evidence

Retain these labelled records: LH/RH before strip; all measurements; cleaned bare backing plates; hub/drum mounting faces; wheel-cylinder markings and port/mount comparison; dry-built spring/adjuster layouts; drums fitted and rotating; completed hard-line/hose/cable routing; bleed/leak test; final adjustment; and post-road-test temperature/leak inspection.
