## What The Photos Show

### 1. Floor-pan corrosion and local perforation

- Multiple floor areas show broad surface rust, pitting, and at least isolated pinholes.
- Rust is concentrated along low points, pressed ribs, bead transitions, and edge returns where moisture has sat under old coatings.
- This is not a full-floor replacement call yet, but it is beyond a simple clean-and-coat job.

### 2. Body-mount and side-seam metal loss

- The tub-side mount/captive-fastener zone shows cracked edges, perforation, and thinning around holes and returns.
- Rust is present in lap/seam interfaces, which means at least part of the loss is trapped between layers rather than only on the exposed face.
- Any spinning, stripped, or weak captive thread in this zone should be treated as a metal rebuild issue, not a hardware-only issue.

### 3. Corner and hinge assembly failure

- The hinge pin had to be cut off.
- The adjacent corner metal is visibly swollen, cracked, and perforated.
- This is the highest-risk zone because it combines rust, alignment, and hardware function in one assembly.

### 4. Aperture and side-shell edge corrosion

- The door/aperture-side shell shows rust at vertical edges, returns, and lower transitions.
- Some of this may stay in the "clean and retain" bucket, but only after the paint, filler, and seam sealer are removed well past the visible staining.

## Repair Standard

- No overlay plates on visible body skin or floor sheet.
- No filler over rust.
- No rust converter trapped inside a seam that is about to be welded closed.
- Any perforated, cracked, or knife-edge-thin metal in a load path or hinge/mount area gets cut out and replaced.
- Every repaired zone must be proven on both faces as far as access allows.

## Material Release

- `1.2 mm` cold-rolled mild steel:
  - floor-pan patch sections
  - small closure patches
  - light skin and lip repairs
- `1.6 mm` cold-rolled mild steel:
  - heavier floor returns
  - edge flanges
  - corner rebuild sections where the original shell is visibly heavier than skin
- `3.0 mm` mild-steel plate:
  - captive-nut backing plates
  - local mount reinforcement coupons
  - hinge-support doubler pieces only where the original part proves to be similar thickness
- Hardware and consumables:
  - replacement captive or weld nuts as required after thread inspection
  - hinge pin stock or replacement hinge hardware if the original bore/pin set is not reusable
  - cutting discs, flap discs, wire cup, weld-through primer for unavoidable closed laps, epoxy primer, seam sealer, and cavity wax

## Work Sequence

### Stage 1. Expose and map everything

1. Strip paint, filler, seam sealer, and loose rust at least `50-75 mm` beyond every visible defect.
2. Tag each zone by exact vehicle station before cutting:
   - floor patch area(s)
   - mount/captive-fastener area(s)
   - hinge/corner assembly
   - aperture edge and lower side-shell returns
3. Probe with pick and hammer, then mark:
   - perforated
   - thin but intact
   - sound after cleanup
4. Photograph every zone after exposure, not just before cleanup.

### Stage 2. Brace and datum the opening

1. Brace the affected opening before the corner or hinge structure is cut apart.
2. Record:
   - hinge spacing
   - pin diameter
   - hinge centerline
   - aperture width/diagonals
3. If the opposite side is intact, use it as the dimensional reference.
4. Keep a temporary alignment rod through hinge knuckles during mock-up and tack-up.

### Stage 3. Rebuild the hinge/corner assembly first

1. Cut out all cracked and perforated hinge-support metal, including any rotten inner box or return hidden behind the outer face.
2. Separate the repair into layers:
   - inner support or box section
   - hinge-support face
   - outer corner or return skin
3. Replace any ovalized, split, or cut-damaged hinge knuckle rather than trying to dress it back into shape.
4. Trial-fit the hinge/pin assembly before final welding.
5. Do not fully finish this zone until the opening shape and hardware movement are proven.

### Stage 4. Rebuild the mount and seam structure

1. Open the seam enough to remove all trapped scale.
2. Re-reference the mount-hole center and captive-nut position before cutting the old plate out.
3. Replace in the same order the factory structure used:
   - backing/reinforcement plate
   - captive-nut or threaded feature
   - outer sheet or return flange
4. Use `3.0 mm` only for the backing/reinforcement layer, not as a substitute for outer shell sheet.
5. Plug-weld back through the flange pattern where the original construction relied on spot-welded layers.

### Stage 5. Repair floor pans and low-point rust

1. After edge structures are stable, decide floor repairs by actual cleaned boundary, not by the rusty stain alone.
2. Any isolated pinhole or thin cluster near a rib, seam, or drain feature should be cut out as a shaped section large enough to reach solid metal.
3. Reproduce beads, steps, and ribs where they add stiffness; do not close them with flat plates if the original pressing can be approximated.
4. Butt-weld flat patches where possible and plug-weld where the floor rejoins an underlying brace or flange.
5. If a floor area remains uniformly solid after cleanup, keep it and coat it rather than replacing sound original steel.

### Stage 6. Clean up aperture-edge and side-shell rust

1. Strip the full vertical edge and lower return until the rust boundary is visible.
2. Localize repairs:
   - edge-lip patch
   - lower return patch
   - small side-skin closure
3. Keep these secondary to the hinge and mount structure, because the edge finish depends on those harder points being correct first.

### Stage 7. Corrosion closure

1. Dress welds only as much as needed for fit and sealing.
2. Blow out dust, degrease, and apply epoxy primer to all bare steel.
3. Seam-seal only after the metal is fully verified and dry.
4. Apply the selected top protection by zone.
5. Flood closed sections and rebuilt seams with cavity wax after primer/topcoat cure.

## Pass/Fail Gates

- No visible perforation remains at any cut edge.
- No cracking remains around hinge supports, mount holes, or seam returns.
- Hinge pin passes through the rebuilt hinge stack without bind.
- Captive threads accept the correct fastener cleanly and hold torque without spinning.
- Floor repairs have no pinholes under backlight or air test.
- Primer covers all repaired bare metal before the shell sits outside or collects workshop moisture again.

## Practical Priorities

### P0: Must be rebuilt, not merely cleaned

- Hinge/corner assembly with the cut hinge pin
- Perforated or cracked body-mount/captive-fastener metal
- Any seam section that opens up or delaminates when cleaned

### P1: Repair after cleanup confirms the final cut lines

- Floor-pan pinholes and thin low-point sections
- Rusted edge returns adjacent to repaired mount or corner zones

### P2: Clean, verify, and retain if sound

- Broad floor surface rust away from perforations
- Aperture-edge staining that cleans back to full thickness

## Unknowns Still To Close

- Exact hidden condition behind the corner/hinge outer face
- Whether the captive-nut structures are reusable or need full replacement
- Whether the floor corrosion is still localized after full stripping, or expands into larger continuous thin sections
- Whether previous body filler or old repairs are hiding wider cut lines

## Recommended Next Action

Run one controlled metal-exposure pass on the hinge/corner zone and one mount/seam zone first. Those two openings will tell you whether the current job remains a localized patch program or needs a larger sectional rebuild package.
