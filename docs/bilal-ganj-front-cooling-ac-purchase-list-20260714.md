# Bilal Ganj Front Cooling and A/C Purchase List - 2026-07-14

Purpose: buy a complete new radiator and A/C condenser installation for the 1978 J40 with Toyota 2H engine, using parts available new in Bilal Ganj and local fabrication. This is the shop-facing specification. The old radiator remains the pattern for neck position, neck angle, cap position, drain, and hose routing.

## Purchase Decision

Buy these two principal components:

1. **Radiator:** new HJ47/2H-pattern copper/brass heavy-duty four-row radiator, Toyota pattern `16400-68030`. The preferred direct-replacement core is `435 mm high x 530 mm wide x 64 mm deep`; an equivalent `435 x 540 x 60 mm` core is acceptable only if the measured upright clearance allows it. Require `38 mm` upper and lower hose necks and no automatic-transmission oil cooler.
2. **A/C condenser:** new universal R134a parallel-flow condenser in the nominal `14 x 22 in` class. The preferred body is approximately `356 mm high x 559 mm wide x 21 mm deep`; the hard installed-body envelope is `370 mm high x 600 mm wide x 25 mm deep`, excluding hose elbows. Because suppliers use "14 x 22" for either the finned core or the overall body, measure both. Require a useful finned face of at least about `330 mm high x 508 mm wide`. Use a `#8` male O-ring inlet high and `#6` male O-ring outlet low, preferably on the same side.

Do not buy a Prado or Fortuner radiator for this installation. Its roughly `648-658 mm` core width and approximately `700 mm` overall width are too large for the estimated `640-670 mm` front opening. Do not use the Corolla 2008-2014 condenser as the default: its proprietary block fittings and wider fitting envelope make a custom hose installation harder. The universal `14 x 22 in` condenser is the better match.

All fluid-path parts in this list are to be **new**. A used radiator, condenser, receiver-drier, hose, cap, thermostat, or unknown A/C fitting from a donor vehicle is not an acceptable substitute. The old radiator is a dimensional sample only.

## Component Arrangement

```text
FRONT OF VEHICLE                                                   ENGINE

grille -> 14 in pusher fan -> 14 x 22 condenser -> 10-15 mm gap
       -> HJ47/2H radiator -> close-fitting fan shroud -> mechanical fan

         fan has its own       condenser has its own      radiator sits on
         hoop/crossbar         forward mounting tabs      two rubber saddles
```

Mount the condenser upright with its side manifolds vertical; do not rotate a parallel-flow condenser flat or 90 degrees from its designed orientation. Put the `#8` inlet high and the `#6` outlet low if the selected condenser is marked for that flow direction. Put the new receiver-drier vertically near the `#6` outlet. Use short-radius or long-radius `90-degree` O-ring fittings as required so the fittings turn into the side cavity rather than projecting outside the `640-670 mm` opening. The seller must trial-fit the actual elbows before payment.

The radiator, condenser, fan, and drier must each be removable separately. No fan or condenser load may be carried through radiator fins, condenser fins, tubes, or tanks.

## Existing Parts to Inspect Before Buying Replacements

- Existing Sanden-type compressor: prove `12 V` clutch, pulley alignment, clutch current, head/port type, shaft seal condition, oil type, and leak condition.
- Existing mechanical fan: reject cracked, repaired, badly corroded, or bent blades; verify fan-to-radiator and fan-to-shroud clearance through engine movement.
- Water pump and pulley: reject bearing play, seal leakage, or pulley runout.
- Thermostat housing and engine-side `38 mm` necks: remove rust and reject deep pitting at clamp lands.
- Purchased under-dash evaporator: identify TXV, thermostat, blower current, #6 inlet, #10 outlet, and drain before hose purchase.
- Alternator and vehicle wiring: confirm capacity for blower, pusher fan, and compressor clutch at hot idle.

## Three-Pass Buying Order

1. **Pass 1 - hard parts:** buy RAD-01 through RAD-07 and AC-01 through AC-05. Take the old radiator sample and a steel tape. Dry-fit radiator, condenser, fan, drier, grille, bonnet latch, and mechanical fan before final bracket welding.
2. **Pass 2 - routes:** after positions are fixed, buy RAD-08 through RAD-18, AC-06 through AC-16, and AC-22 through AC-25 plus fabrication hardware. Select every hose angle and both V-belts on the vehicle. Obtain an exchange agreement for unused fittings.
3. **Pass 3 - commissioning:** after compressor/evaporator inspection, buy the confirmed oil, refrigerant, TXV/freeze-control items, coolant, fuses, and final electrical consumables. Crimp hoses only now.

## Seller Acceptance Checklist

Do not pay for the principal components until the seller/shop has done the following:

- Put a steel tape across radiator core width, height, and depth and across overall mounting width; photograph every reading.
- Put the old radiator beside the new radiator and match both `38 mm` neck ODs, neck directions, cap location, overflow, drain, and mounting relationship.
- Bench pressure-test the radiator and provide the test result; fins must be straight and tanks/mount rails unstressed.
- Measure the condenser body and mounting rails. Fit the actual `#8` and `#6` elbows and prove they remain inside the vehicle side envelope.
- Confirm the condenser ports were supplied capped and the receiver-drier is factory sealed. Do not open the drier for display or mock-up.
- Bench-run the fan as a pusher, mark airflow direction, and record running current. Check that the blade does not flex into the condenser.
- Give a written list of every hose fitting thread and hose size before crimping.
- Agree that final hose crimping happens only after the installed mock-up is approved.

## Installation and Commissioning Gates

The job is not complete until all of these pass:

1. Radiator sits square on two rubber lower saddles and is retained on both sides without twist or hose load.
2. Condenser has independent mounts and a measured `10-15 mm` gap to the radiator.
3. Fan has independent frame mounts, pushes air rearward, and does not use through-fin rods.
4. Mechanical fan, belts, bonnet latch, grille, drain, cap, and hose-service access all remain clear.
5. Cooling system is flushed with clean water and given a final distilled-water rinse, filled with a reputable corrosion-inhibited coolant compatible with copper/brass and cast iron, bled to thermostat opening, leak-checked, and pressure-tested. Buy `16 L` of prepared `50/50` coolant, or `8 L` concentrate plus sufficient distilled water, so a roughly 15-litre system and recovery bottle can be filled without mixing products. Also buy `20 L` distilled/de-mineralized water for final rinsing. Record the actual converted-vehicle refill volume.
6. A/C system is nitrogen pressure/leak-tested, evacuated for at least `30 minutes` after repair, vacuum-hold tested, and charged with R134a by weight by the A/C technician.
7. Trinary low/high cutout, condenser-fan request, compressor relay, fuses, grounds, and hot-idle operation are tested.
8. Final evidence records hot-idle coolant behavior, fan clearance, A/C high/low pressures, ambient temperature, center-vent temperature, fan current, and compressor clutch current.
